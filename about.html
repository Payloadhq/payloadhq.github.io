About Payload

Payload develops innovative software and applications that simplify operations, solve practical challenges, and help businesses and individuals work more efficiently.

An independent software company

Payload is an independent software development company. It designs, builds, and sells its own software: every product in the catalog is conceived, engineered, priced, and supported by Payload itself, and sold directly from this site. There is no client work and no agency model behind the catalog.

Why Payload exists

Complex work usually breaks at the seams. A payment an agent should never have made. A payout nobody can verify. A system migration poisoned by duplicate records. A server launched before anyone checked what it exposes. Payload builds the tools that sit at those seams: narrow, well-defined software that removes a specific operational problem and stays out of the way of everything else.

How Payload works

Problem first

Every product starts from a concrete, recurring problem, not a feature list. If the problem is not real and repeated, the product does not get built.

Honest scope

Product pages state what the software does and what it does not do. Several tools run fully offline, because the work they do does not need a server. Where a product has limits, the limits are written down before you pay, not after.

Plain pricing

One-time licenses or clear subscriptions. The price on the page is the final price at checkout, and every purchase is a direct sale from Payload through Stripe.

Support from the builders

Support comes from the people who built the product. Questions are answered by someone who can open the code, not by a script.

Built to be used

Software only helps if people can actually use it. Payload products favor clear interfaces over clever ones, documentation and guides written around the task you are trying to finish, and free sandboxes, samples, and utilities wherever they help you judge the fit before paying.

A growing ecosystem

The catalog holds eleven products across seven categories today: agent payments and commerce infrastructure, revenue rules and royalties, MCP security and launch readiness, AI search and discoverability, data cleanup and migration, automation and agent reliability, and finance recordkeeping, alongside free guides and utilities. The ecosystem grows one well-defined problem at a time.
