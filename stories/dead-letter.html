Paid in Full. Nothing Arrived.

Story type: a synthetic case built from a documented failure class, published as the first case on Payload's Dead Letter Exchange board.

An agent pays for an API result through x402. The facilitator verifies the payment authorization: valid. The settlement transaction lands on chain, and anyone can read it back: the right token, the right amount, the right recipient, a transaction hash, a block, a log index. By every measure the payment layer offers, the purchase is complete.

The purchased result never arrives. When the agent asks for it again, presenting the settled payment, the endpoint answers with a fresh demand to pay, as if the first payment did not exist. The operator checks the seller's own delivery record for the order. It returns 404.

The agent's framework, doing exactly what it was built to do, prepares to run the payment flow again from the top.

Three different facts that get treated as one

Payment systems chain together facts that feel like a single event and are not:

  1. Verification says a payment authorization is well formed and acceptable. It moves no money.
  2. Settlement says value actually moved on chain, from the buyer to the named recipient, in the named amount. The locator triple (transaction hash, block number, log index) proves exactly this and nothing more.
  3. Fulfillment says the buyer received what the payment was for. No field in a settlement proves it. It lives in a different system: the seller's delivery logic and delivery records.

The dead letter class lives in the gap between the second and third facts. The public x402 ecosystem has spent real threads working through the gap between the first and second (verification mistaken for settlement). One step further downstream sits the quieter failure: settlement mistaken for delivery. Everything the buyer can easily check says success. The only thing missing is the thing they bought.

What the evidence can and cannot establish

In a case like this, a careful read of the transcript establishes a short, solid list: a challenge was served with specific terms; an authorization matched those terms; verification passed; a settlement matching the challenge landed on chain; a delivery attempt presenting that settlement was answered with a new payment demand; the seller's delivery record for the order did not exist; no second payment was made.

The same evidence does not establish why. It does not show whether the seller's delivery step is broken, misconfigured, or deliberately withholding. It does not show what the parties agreed about refunds. It does not show anything that happened off the recorded channels. An analysis that claims more than the fields support is not an analysis; it is a guess with a receipt attached, and it falls apart the moment the buyer takes it to the counterparty.

The dangerous next step

The failure also manufactures its own trap. The endpoint's fresh 402 looks like an instruction: pay again to get your result. An agent framework on an automatic retry does not deliberate; it pays. Now the buyer has settled twice for one undelivered result, and the incident has quietly doubled. The safe sequence is boring and strict: stop the retry, preserve the full transcript, put the settlement locator triple in front of the seller, and ask for delivery or reconciliation against that specific payment. The evidence pack is the leverage. A second payment is just a second loss.
